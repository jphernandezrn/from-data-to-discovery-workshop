Questionnaire Title

Clinical Simulation Confidence and Skill Questionnaire

Theoretical Framework

Develop the questionnaire according to the following nursing theories:

• Dorothy Johnson - Behavioral System Model • Imogene M. King - Theory
of Goal Attainment • Dorothea Orem - Self-Care Deficit Nursing Theory •
Faye G. Abdellah - Twenty-One Nursing Problems Theory

Questionnaire Structure

Generate exactly 15 questionnaire items organized into the following
subscales.

Part A. Managing Behavior in Simulation (Johnson) - 4 items

Part B. Collaborative Decision-Making (King) - 3 items

Part C. Clinical Readiness and Independence (Orem) - 4 items

Part D. Problem Recognition and Prioritization (Abdellah) - 4 items

Generate original, concise, unbiased, and academically appropriate
statements. Each item must measure only one construct and should avoid
leading, double-barreled, or ambiguous wording.

The questionnaire should be appropriate for undergraduate and graduate
nursing students participating in clinical simulation.

Response Scale

Use a 7-point Likert scale for every questionnaire item.

1 = Strongly disagree 2 = Disagree 3 = Somewhat disagree 4 = Neither
agree nor disagree 5 = Somewhat agree 6 = Agree 7 = Strongly agree

Scoring

-   Total questionnaire items: 15
-   Possible total score range: 15 to 105

Interpretation Guide

Use the following interpretation exactly.

• High = 83 to 105 • Moderate = 53 to 82 • Low = 15 to 52

Conceptual Framework

Independent Variable (IV): Clinical Readiness and Independence (Orem)

Mediator: Managing Behavior in Simulation (Johnson)

Moderator: Collaborative Decision-Making (King)

Dependent Variable (DV): Clinical Simulation Confidence and Skill

Confounder: Previous Clinical Simulation Experience

Covariate: Year Level

Ensure exactly one IV, one DV, one mediator, one moderator, one
confounder, and one covariate.
